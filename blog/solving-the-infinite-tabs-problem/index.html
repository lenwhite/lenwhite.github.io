Solving the infinite tabs problem

July 13, 2026·
  • productivity

If you're terminally online like me, your tabs multiply like rabbits during deer season.

Each tab is a deferred decision, part of a backlog that never clears. There will always be more claims on your attention than you can satisfy.

Generalize this to everything in life, and you rederive the task management and prioritization problem.

At one extreme, you track nothing. Inbox, feed, notifications, and open tabs determine where attention goes. You spend your time firefighting or consuming whatever appears next.

At the other extreme, you track everything in a second brain. Every recommendation, passing interest, and possible project enters a system. Each item gets categorized, tagged, reviewed, and preserved. The system becomes a museum of unrealized intentions, and maintaining it consumes the time it was meant to protect.

Ironically, digital tools push us toward this extreme. Bookmarking something on Goodreads or a read-later app takes a second. There is no friction and no filter.

Writing by hand enforced discipline on what got written. Its slowness invited reflection. Only what survived the pause made it onto the page. On a computer, typing outpaces thought. The selection that handwriting imposed must become deliberate.

A useful system sits somewhere between the two extremes. Its position depends on one question:

What does it cost to forget this?

Missing an appointment has consequences. Forgetting a film recommendation does not.

Commitments need durable capture. Active projects need review. Possibilities need curation. Casual interests can decay.

The operating rule:

Track commitments. Curate possibilities. Forget the rest.

A list is not a promise. A reading list is not a queue that must reach zero. A second brain should preserve options, not manufacture obligations.

For me, this means logging ideas and possibilities in one place, then ruthlessly cutting the rest. Commitments live in a calendar and task manager. Possibilities earn their continued place. Everything else disappears.

Use whatever system fits—bullet journal, GTD, whatever. At their core, they reflect the same choice: deciding which intentions deserve to survive.
