In hopes of an AI plateau

September 20, 2026·
  • AI

My take: a natural, temporary plateau in AI progress is the best case for humanity.

There's one world where AI development stays a race to the bottom. Models get more powerful until we hit recursive self-improvement. A cybersecurity catastrophe — from an open model, a frontier lab, or a government lab — does critical damage to global internet infrastructure. If we survive, the world overreacts and clamps down on AI progress for a long time.

There's another world where AI development is stillborn. Amid calls for regulatory capture and fear-mongering, industry groups self-regulate or the US and China mandate it by law. The technology is strangled in its crib, gated to large corporations and governments, doled out in watered-down dribs and drabs out of fear of the four horsemen of the infocalypse.

The world I hope for is a world where we continue as now — US frontier labs leading, open models a half-step behind, the science and techniques replicable. Then the labs slow and stop as the low-hanging fruit runs out. We reach a plateau[1] where models are dangerous but survivable, where open models catch up, and where we get used to increasingly capable open models with cyber capabilities in personal hands.

We want the public used to strong, cyber-capable models acting in their users' interests — the norm created in a world where bad guys with guns force everyone to arm up and harden their own infrastructure, second-amendment style, rather than drift into the complacency of a world with no guns. Use this period to build institutional knowledge: battle-tested laws against their misuse rather than preemptive, speculative ones that do too much and too little. Let the average joe develop some intuition for their capabilities, and a healthy respect — the kind you'd have for a loaded firearm or a bomb.[2]

So when the next jump comes — the one to true AGI — it's less of an outside context problem, and we're readier for it.

  1. The plateau might come from LLMs' inability to update their own knowledge or behavior post-training. Or their inability to navigate the physical world, with robotics gating progress there. Or from running out of training data and compute to throw at the problem. ↩

  2. The analogy breaks down here — hardening everything against firearms and bombs, school-shooter-drill style, is stupid and infeasible. Hardening your IT infrastructure is common sense with no real downside. ↩
